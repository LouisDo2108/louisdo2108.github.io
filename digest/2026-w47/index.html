Week 47 · 16–22 Nov 2026

5 new papers · 3 to revise · Mon 16 Nov – Sun 22 Nov 2026

Curated weekly with AI assistance. Summaries can contain mistakes, so check the paper before citing. Figures are reproduced for review with credit to the authors; contact me to have one removed.

trend of the week — Re-using what you already have is the common thread this week: Google projects queries from a new encoder into an existing index instead of re-embedding the corpus, a fair, identifier-expanded BM25 erases most of the dense advantage in code search, and CMU distils students from the teacher's saved GRPO rollouts instead of regenerating data. Agents and world models are also learning from their own experience: frontier LLMs organise their own self-improvement loops, and JEPA world models reuse an observed outcome's residual to answer "what if" questions.

Precise Zero-Shot Dense Retrieval without Relevance Labels

Carnegie Mellon University, University of Waterloo

Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics

HyDE: an LLM writes a hypothetical answer document and its embedding is used as the query, the generate-then-retrieve idea that this week's generate-then-grep expansion for BM25 code search applies with identifiers.

Reflexion: Language Agents with Verbal Reinforcement Learning

Agents improve by writing verbal reflections on failures into memory instead of updating weights, the human-designed context-optimisation loop that this week's MIT paper asks whether capable agents can now invent for themselves.

Retrieval & recommendation

arXiv 2026

ERA: Embed, Route, Adapt for Cold-Start Cross-Model Latent Retrieval

Google

arXiv preprint

Serves queries from a cheap encoder against an existing index built by another model without re-embedding the corpus: anchors chosen by cosine farthest-point sampling, an adapter trained with directional alignment plus orthogonal regularisation, and a router that escalates hard queries to the original encoder (up to +35% relative nDCG@10 on ArguAna).

use it now → When swapping or downsizing a query encoder, embed a few farthest-point-sampled anchor documents with both models and train a small linear adapter with an orthogonality regulariser instead of re-indexing.

arXiv 2026

Embeddings vs. Grep: Rethinking the Dense Advantage in Code Search

Ant Group

arXiv preprint

A repository-level code-search benchmark (RepoPremium) shows much of the dense-over-BM25 gap comes from evaluation choices (chunked BM25, counting edited tests) and queries missing the code's rare identifiers; expanding queries with LLM-written identifiers lets BM25 match dense retrieval on every Python pool.

use it now → Before claiming a dense gain, compare against tuned whole-file BM25, fuse dense with BM25, and expand queries with LLM-generated identifiers (generate-then-grep).

Agents & agentic RL

arXiv 2026

Do LLM Agents Still Need to Be Told How to Self-Improve?

MIT CSAIL, University of Tübingen

arXiv preprint

Gives a frozen frontier LLM only Read/Write/Bash/Gameplay tools, a budget and the goal of getting better at puzzle games; strong models invent their own curricula (writing practice and adversarial games) and context updates that transfer to held-out games and 3D navigation, with no prescribed self-improvement loop.

Reinforcement learning

NeurIPS 2026

Don't Discard Your Rollouts: Reusing Teacher RL Traces for Student Distillation

Carnegie Mellon University

Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems

Distils students from the rollouts already saved during a teacher's GRPO run: easy prompts (by pass rate) for an SFT warm start, mixed-success prompts for DPO, and the teacher's saved log-likelihoods to pick student-compatible completions; matches canonical distillation on maths and code with no extra generation.

use it now → Log prompt pass rates and per-completion log-likelihoods during GRPO so the rollouts can later be reused to distil smaller models.

World models

arXiv 2026

Counterfactual World Models: JEPAs That Reason About "What If"

Brown University, New York University, AMI Labs

arXiv preprint

After observing an outcome, adds the observed residual to the factual model's prediction for another action to estimate what would have happened under the same hidden disturbance; no new training, with error bounds for short transitions, and counterfactual R² rising from about 40% to 86–96% in simulated control tasks.
